# The Effect of Structured Templates on Document Comprehension

**Authors:** Research Group, Department of Information Science
**Date:** June 2026

## Abstract

This study examines whether professionally structured document templates improve reader comprehension and retention compared with unformatted text. In a controlled experiment (n = 240), participants read technical material presented either as plain text or under a structured standard with consistent typography and hierarchy. Comprehension scores improved by 18% in the structured condition, with the largest gains among non-expert readers.

## 1. Introduction

Document structure has long been hypothesised to act as a scaffold for comprehension. While prior work has focused on typography in isolation, few studies have measured the combined effect of hierarchy, spacing and tabular presentation on reader performance.

## 2. Methodology

Participants were randomly assigned to one of two conditions:

1. **Control:** unformatted continuous text.
2. **Treatment:** the same content under a professional document standard.

Comprehension was measured through a 20-item recall test administered immediately after reading and again after seven days.

## 3. Results

| Measure | Control | Treatment | Difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate recall | 61% | 74% | +13 pts |
| Delayed recall (7 days) | 42% | 57% | +15 pts |
| Reading time | 14.2 min | 12.8 min | −10% |

## 4. Discussion

The treatment group read faster and retained more, consistent with the hypothesis that structural cues reduce cognitive load. Notably, the effect persisted at seven days, suggesting that structure aids encoding rather than merely search.

## 5. Conclusion

Professional document structure is not cosmetic: it measurably improves comprehension and retention. Future work should isolate the contribution of individual structural elements.
